## Using the Transaction Checklist

Checklists keep earnest money, HOA, objections, and lender milestones from slipping through crack-of-busy. Treat them as living—not ceremonial.

### Auto-complete behavior

Some items complete themselves when the document story catches up—examples your admin might enable:

1. A recorded **approved** status on a linked document when DocuSign completes.
2. Title milestones when a commitment uploads—or similar integrations your brokerage wires.

If something auto-completes unexpectedly, **verify the underlying artifact** before calling it done.

> **Gold rule:** Automation saves clicks—not accountability. Eyes still belong on material facts.

### Manual completion

When you’ve verified something without a machine cue:

1. Toggle the item once you’ve confirmed against source—PDF, email confirmation, or MLS evidence as policy requires.
2. If you toggle in error, flip it back—your audit trail matters less than correctness.

### Due dates

Where due dates exist:

1. Use realistic dates tied to contract timelines—not every item needs tomorrow’s urgency.
2. Teach assistants that moving dates without narrative causes chaos—note why when you shift.

### PDF export

If your workspace exports checklist summaries:

1. Export after a milestone—preliminary HUD review, lender conditions cleared, final walk photos uploaded—whatever your office uses for file QA.
2. Name exports with **property + milestone** so recipients open the right packet.

> **Remember:** Checklists mirror **risk**, not busywork—what’s on the list should map to something your broker-in-charge would defend.
