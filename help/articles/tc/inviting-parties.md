## Inviting Parties to the Transaction

Invites set expectations before someone asks “why can’t I see that?” in all caps. Send them early enough to be useful—late enough that facts aren’t fiction.

### How to invite each role

1. From the transaction file, open **Parties** (or your tenant’s equivalent).
2. Choose **Invite** beside the role—**buyer**, **seller**, **listing agent**, **buyer agent**, **lender**, **title**, or **coordinator** slots your template exposes.
3. Enter the **work email** your policy requires—personal Gmail invites often break brokerage compliance rules.
4. Send. The recipient accepts, sets a password (or SSO if configured), and lands in a **scoped** workspace.

> **Tip:** Invite coordinators and agents before consumers when paperwork is still volatile—fewer confused homeowners.

### What each party can see

By design, people see **their transaction slice**, not your whole pipeline:

- **Buyers / sellers** — Milestones, documents shared with them, messages on their file—**not** other clients’ deals.
- **Agents** — Collaboration views tied to representation—typically broader than consumers, narrower than internal TC admin.
- **Mortgage / title** — Conditions and docs relevant to their lane—**not** unrelated brokerage chatter unless you explicitly share.

> **Important:** Row-level security is your safety rail—don’t screenshot confidential packets into email when NEXA already scoped access.

### Resending invites

1. Find the party row—status usually shows **pending**.
2. Choose **Resend invite**—don’t create duplicate party rows with the same email; it fractures audit trails.

### Revoking access

When someone leaves the deal—or you invited the wrong address:

1. Use **Remove access** or your tenant’s revoke control on that party row.
2. Confirm downstream—signatures in flight may still complete depending on provider settings.

> **Warm clarity:** If you revoke a buyer’s spouse accidentally, apologize fast and re-invite—stress shows up as mistakes at COE.
