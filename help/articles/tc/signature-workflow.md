## Sending Documents for Signature

E-sign should feel inevitable—correct recipients, clean PDFs, signing order that matches how your brokerage negotiates (sign buyer before seller, or per attorney guidance).

### Preparing a document

1. Finalize the PDF on your side first—no half-filled placeholders unless everyone expects counters.
2. Upload or select the packet within the transaction’s document manager.
3. Confirm **page order**—especially exhibits and HOA amendments that riders reference.

> **Gold rule:** Wire instructions never belong in the signing packet—send those through verified channels only.

### Placing signature blocks

In DocuSign (or your configured provider):

1. Drop fields where signers actually sign—initials on notice pages, full signatures on contract pages as law requires.
2. Align dates **signer-local** where applicable—your provider handles locale formatting.

### Selecting signers

1. Map recipients to roles your brokerage expects—buyer, seller, broker associates where applicable.
2. Set routing **order** deliberately—sequential when finance must see executed buyer docs before seller counters, parallel when independent.

> **Remember:** Signers only see what you send in that envelope—double-check attachments before you launch.

### What the signer sees

They’ll get an email with a secure link—inside:

1. Page-by-page navigation (mobile-friendly on good templates).
2. Required fields highlighted—less room for “I thought I signed everything.”

### After signing

1. Watch document status move toward **completed** in NEXA when webhooks are configured.
2. Download the final composite PDF for your file copy—not just “it looked green in DocuSign.”

### Voiding envelopes

When something’s wrong mid-flight:

1. Void from the provider with a **clear reason** recorded—your audit trail should read professional, not cryptic.
2. Upload the corrected PDF and **send a fresh envelope**—avoid endless “replace page 7” ping-pong without version discipline.

> **Important:** Voiding doesn’t unsay email—call conflict-prone parties when wires or identities were wrong.
