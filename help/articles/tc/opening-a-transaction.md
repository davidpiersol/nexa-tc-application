## Opening a New Transaction

Opening a file cleanly saves ten correction emails later. Follow your brokerage’s numbering rules—but inside NEXA, think in terms of **identifiers**, **people**, **documents**, and **First Pass**.

### Step-by-step: New Transaction through First Pass

1. Click **New Transaction** from your TC workspace entry point (often the Transactions list or dashboard).
2. Enter what identifies the property—**MLS number**, **street address**, and deal side **purchase / listing / lease** if your template asks for it.
3. Save the draft. With an MLS number on file, **AI First Pass** can pull listing context automatically—give it a beat to run before assuming nothing happened.
4. Open the transaction file. Confirm parties look correct before inviting anyone (agents first, then buyers/sellers as policy dictates).
5. Upload the core packet—contract or amendment—as soon as you have a signed PDF (even if subject to counters later).
6. Go to **First Pass**. Review what the model surfaced against your uploaded PDF and MLS pull.

### What confidence percentages mean

The confidence ring is a **review prioritizer**, not a verdict.

1. **Higher percentages** — The model aligned fields cleanly across sources (MLS, public record pulls where enabled, your uploaded PDF text).
2. **Lower percentages** — Read carefully—often handwriting, scans, addenda not yet indexed, or conflicting dates across documents.

Nothing here replaces your judgment or brokerage compliance review.

> **Gold rule:** When confidence is low on **dates** or **money**, slow down before routing outward—those fields propagate everywhere downstream.

### Approving First Pass

When you’re satisfied:

1. Walk the verification checklist item by item—toggle items as you confirm against source docs.
2. Move First Pass status toward **approved** using your office convention (may be a button or status change—follow training).
3. Route documents next—send for signature, distribute copies to title/lender as your checklist dictates.

### After approval

1. Notify parties only after material facts look stable—avoid “approval churn.”
2. Keep the transaction email inbox monitored if your office uses email-ingestion—clients love to reply-all.

> **Important:** First Pass helps you **start sharp**—it doesn’t replace earnest deposits timing, HOA timelines, or jurisdiction-specific forms.
